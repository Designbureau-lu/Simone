                PRESENTATION
            
    
        
    
        
    
            
    This presentation traces Simone Decker's artistic journey from one of her earliest exhibitions in this very museum in 1994 to the present day. Thirty-two years later, she was awarded the Lëtzebuerger Konschtpräis by the Ministry of Culture, recognising her remarkable contribution to contemporary art, both in Luxembourg and internationally. To mark this occasion, the museum’s Lëtzebuerger Konschtarchiv presents an overview of her work across three decades.
    
            
    Simone Decker (born 1968 in Luxembourg) invites us to see familiar places in unexpected ways. Like opening a curtain, her work encourages us to look beyond the obvious. Through her photography and interventions in public space, she uses humour, surprise and subtle shifts in perspective to question how we look at the world around us. Above all, architecture and urban spaces become starting points for exploration and (re)discovery.
    
            
    Discovery lies at the heart of Decker's practice. Keep an eye out for models, prototypes and fragments of the artist’s works displayed throughout the museum’s five underground levels as part of the permanent archaeology exhibition.
    
            
    Level -1: Chewing gum; model for Genus (to be confirmed); prototype for Ghosts
    
            
    Levels -2/-3: Screening of the video Airbag (1998)
    
            
    Level -4: Cellophane used for Pavillons de Saint-Nazaire; element from the installation Le grand soufflé
    
            
    Level -5: Coat from Whitening; one element from the installation Inlays; model for the installation 500 pièces

    The Lëtzebuerger Konschtpräis is the biennial art award, established in 2022 by Luxembourg’s Ministry for Culture. The prize includes €10,000 and honours an artist for their work and ongoing commitment to Luxembourg’s art scene. The artist must be Luxembourgish, live in Luxembourg or have a strong connection with the country. After Berthe Lutgen received the prize in 2022 and Marc-Henri Reckinger in 2024, a professional jury selected Simone Decker as the 2026 laureate.
